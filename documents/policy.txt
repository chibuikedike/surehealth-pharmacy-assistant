# SECTION 1: STORE HOURS AND EMERGENCY PROTOCOLS

## 1.1 Standard Operating Hours

The pharmacy shall maintain the following standard operating schedule to ensure consistent patient access, medication dispensing availability, and operational compliance.

| Day       | Operating Hours   |
| --------- | ----------------- |
| Monday    | 8:00 AM - 9:00 PM |
| Tuesday   | 8:00 AM - 9:00 PM |
| Wednesday | 8:00 AM - 9:00 PM |
| Thursday  | 8:00 AM - 9:00 PM |
| Friday    | 8:00 AM - 9:00 PM |
| Saturday  | 9:00 AM - 6:00 PM |
| Sunday    | Closed            |

All employees must adhere to scheduled opening and closing procedures. Staff members assigned to opening duties must arrive sufficiently early to complete security checks, temperature monitoring verification, controlled medication storage inspection, and dispensing system readiness checks.

Closing procedures must include:

* Verification that all prescriptions requiring pharmacist review have been completed or appropriately transferred.
* Secure storage confirmation for controlled substances and temperature-sensitive medications.
* Documentation of unresolved patient inquiries.
* Reconciliation of cash, insurance claims, and electronic transactions.
* Activation of security systems after final staff departure.

---

# 1.2 Holiday Operating Exceptions

The pharmacy recognizes that public holidays may require modified operating schedules. Holiday changes must be communicated to patients through approved communication channels, including:

* Physical notices displayed at the pharmacy entrance.
* Official website announcements.
* Automated telephone messaging.
* Patient notification systems where available.

Holiday schedules may include:

## Reduced Operating Hours

During selected holidays, the pharmacy may operate under reduced hours to maintain essential medication access while allowing staff availability.

Examples:

* Opening at 10:00 AM instead of 8:00 AM.
* Closing earlier than standard hours.
* Restricting non-essential administrative services.

## Full Closure Days

Certain holidays may require complete closure due to:

* Regulatory requirements.
* Reduced healthcare demand.
* Staff safety considerations.
* National pharmacy operating restrictions.

Patients requiring urgent medication access during closure periods must be directed toward approved emergency pharmacy services or healthcare facilities.

---

# 1.3 Holiday Medication Continuity Procedures

Before scheduled holiday closures, pharmacy staff must:

1. Review upcoming prescription refill requirements.
2. Identify patients with chronic medication dependencies.
3. Provide appropriate refill reminders.
4. Escalate urgent medication access concerns to the supervising pharmacist.

Priority attention must be given to patients receiving:

* Antihypertensive medications.
* Diabetes management medications.
* Anticoagulant therapies.
* Seizure management medications.
* Other medications where interruption may present significant clinical risk.

The pharmacy must not delay medically necessary treatment due to avoidable operational scheduling issues.

---

# 1.4 Emergency Contact Procedures for Pharmacists

A pharmacist must be available through approved emergency communication channels during operational hours.

Emergency pharmacist contact procedures include:

## Step 1: Initial Assessment

The staff member receiving the emergency concern must:

* Identify themselves.
* Record the patient name and relevant identifying information.
* Document the nature of the emergency.
* Determine whether immediate pharmacist intervention is required.

## Step 2: Pharmacist Notification

The responsible pharmacist must be contacted using:

* Official pharmacy communication system.
* Emergency staff contact list.
* Approved escalation pathway.

Personal communication channels must only be used when authorized by management.

## Step 3: Clinical Assessment

The pharmacist must determine:

* Whether the concern relates to medication safety.
* Whether adverse drug reactions are suspected.
* Whether emergency medical referral is required.
* Whether additional documentation is necessary.

## Step 4: Documentation

All emergency interventions must include:

* Date and time of incident.
* Patient details.
* Medication involved.
* Clinical concern.
* Action taken.
* Pharmacist decision.
* Follow-up requirements.

---

# 1.5 Emergency Escalation Categories

## Category 1: Medication Safety Emergency

Examples:

* Incorrect medication supplied.
* Suspected medication interaction.
* Serious adverse reaction.
* Wrong patient medication dispensing.

Required action:

* Immediate pharmacist involvement.
* Patient safety assessment.
* Documentation.
* Regulatory reporting when required.

---

## Category 2: Operational Emergency

Examples:

* Pharmacy computer system failure.
* Refrigeration failure affecting medication storage.
* Security incident.
* Power interruption.

Required action:

* Notify pharmacy manager.
* Secure medication inventory.
* Activate contingency procedures.

---

## Category 3: Medical Emergency

Examples:

* Patient collapse inside pharmacy.
* Severe allergic reaction symptoms.
* Acute medical distress.

Required action:

* Contact emergency medical services.
* Provide immediate assistance within professional scope.
* Document incident after stabilization.

---

# 1.6 Pharmacist On-Call Rotation Policy

The pharmacy shall maintain an organized pharmacist on-call structure to ensure continuity of professional oversight.

## On-Call Responsibilities

The assigned pharmacist is responsible for:

* Urgent medication-related consultations.
* Clinical clarification requests.
* Emergency prescription concerns.
* Staff escalation support.
* Patient safety decisions.

---

## Rotation Scheduling

The pharmacist on-call schedule must:

* Be prepared at least one month in advance.
* Clearly identify primary and backup pharmacists.
* Avoid excessive consecutive assignments.
* Consider workload distribution.

---

## Handover Requirements

Outgoing on-call pharmacists must provide:

* Outstanding patient cases.
* Pending clinical reviews.
* Medication supply concerns.
* Regulatory issues requiring follow-up.

Incoming pharmacists must confirm receipt of handover information.

# SECTION 2: COMPREHENSIVE DOSAGE AND MEDICATION GUIDELINES

## 2.1 General Patient Intake and Medication Assessment Policy

Before dispensing medication, pharmacy personnel must collect appropriate patient information.

Required patient assessment information includes:

* Patient identification.
* Age.
* Weight when clinically relevant.
* Allergy history.
* Current medication list.
* Existing medical conditions.
* Pregnancy or breastfeeding status when applicable.
* Previous medication reactions.

The pharmacist must evaluate potential risks including:

* Drug-drug interactions.
* Contraindications.
* Duplicate therapy.
* Incorrect dosing patterns.
* Patient understanding of administration instructions.

---

# 2.2 Antibiotic Medication Guidance

## Therapeutic Category

Antibiotics are prescription medications used for treatment of bacterial infections. Appropriate use requires adherence to prescribed duration and dosing instructions.

Examples include:

* Amoxicillin.
* Azithromycin.

---

# Amoxicillin Patient Administration Guidelines

## Administration Instructions

Patients should:

* Take medication exactly as prescribed.
* Maintain consistent timing between doses.
* Take with food if gastrointestinal discomfort occurs.
* Complete the entire prescribed course unless directed otherwise by a healthcare professional.

## Patient Counseling Points

Patients must be informed:

* Symptom improvement does not always indicate infection elimination.
* Stopping early may contribute to treatment failure.
* Missed doses should be managed according to pharmacist guidance.

## Monitoring Requirements

Monitor for:

* Allergic reactions.
* Gastrointestinal disturbances.
* Unexpected worsening symptoms.

Patients experiencing serious reactions require immediate clinical assessment.

---

# Azithromycin Administration Guidelines

## Administration Instructions

Patients should:

* Follow prescribed dosing schedule.
* Complete the recommended course.
* Maintain adherence even if symptoms improve.

## Clinical Considerations

Pharmacists should review:

* Cardiac history where relevant.
* Potential medication interactions.
* Previous antibiotic reactions.

---

# 2.3 Analgesics and NSAID Medication Guidelines

Therapeutic category includes:

* Paracetamol.
* Ibuprofen.

---

# Paracetamol Safety Guidelines

## Administration Requirements

Patients must follow:

* Recommended dosing instructions.
* Maximum daily limits provided by healthcare professionals or product labeling.

Patients must avoid exceeding maximum 24-hour limits because excessive intake may increase risk of serious liver injury.

---

## Liver Safety Warnings

Additional caution is required for patients with:

* Existing liver disease.
* Heavy alcohol consumption.
* Multiple medications containing paracetamol.

Patients should review all combination products to avoid accidental duplicate dosing.

---

# Ibuprofen Safety Guidelines

## Administration Requirements

Ibuprofen should generally be taken according to product instructions and may be taken with food to reduce stomach irritation.

---

## Stomach and Bleeding Risk Warnings

Patients must be assessed for:

* History of stomach ulcers.
* Gastrointestinal bleeding risk.
* Kidney problems.
* Concurrent medications affecting bleeding risk.

---

## NSAID Monitoring

Patients should seek medical advice if experiencing:

* Persistent stomach discomfort.
* Signs of medication intolerance.
* Unusual symptoms after use.

# SECTION 2: COMPREHENSIVE DOSAGE AND MEDICATION GUIDELINES (CONTINUED)

# 2.4 Antihistamine Medication Guidelines

## Therapeutic Category Overview

Antihistamines are medications primarily used for management of allergic conditions including:

* Allergic rhinitis.
* Seasonal allergies.
* Urticaria-related symptoms.
* Allergy-associated itching.

Common examples include:

* Cetirizine.
* Loratadine.

Pharmacy personnel must assess patient needs, allergy severity, concurrent medication use, and potential impairment risks before recommending or dispensing non-prescription antihistamines.

---

# Cetirizine Administration Guidelines

## Medication Classification

Cetirizine is classified as a second-generation antihistamine. Although generally associated with reduced sedation compared with older antihistamines, some patients may still experience drowsiness.

---

## Patient Administration Instructions

Patients should:

* Follow the recommended dosage instructions.
* Take consistently according to the prescribed or labelled schedule.
* Avoid exceeding recommended daily limits.
* Monitor personal response before engaging in activities requiring full alertness.

---

## Drowsiness and Sedation Precautions

Patients must be informed that:

* Individual responses vary.
* Some individuals may experience sleepiness or reduced concentration.
* Alcohol consumption may increase impairment risks.

Patients should avoid driving, operating machinery, or performing safety-sensitive tasks until they understand how the medication affects them.

---

# Loratadine Administration Guidelines

## Medication Classification

Loratadine is a second-generation antihistamine commonly described as having a lower likelihood of causing drowsiness compared with first-generation antihistamines.

---

## Patient Counseling Requirements

Patients should understand:

* Reduced sedation does not mean zero sedation risk.
* Personal sensitivity differs.
* Combining multiple allergy medications without professional advice may create unnecessary duplication.

---

# Antihistamine Safety Assessment

Before recommending antihistamines, pharmacy staff should evaluate:

* Patient age.
* Existing medical conditions.
* Current medication list.
* Previous antihistamine reactions.
* Requirement for alertness during work or daily activities.

---

# 2.5 Chronic Care Medication Management Guidelines

## Chronic Medication Overview

Chronic medications require consistent adherence because treatment effectiveness depends on maintaining stable therapeutic management.

Examples include:

* Metformin.
* Lisinopril.

Pharmacy staff must reinforce medication continuity, monitoring requirements, and appropriate follow-up.

---

# Metformin Patient Management Guidelines

## Therapeutic Purpose

Metformin is commonly prescribed as part of diabetes management strategies.

---

## Administration Principles

Patients should:

* Take medication consistently as prescribed.
* Maintain adherence even when symptoms are not noticeable.
* Follow healthcare provider instructions regarding timing and food intake.

Taking medication with meals may improve gastrointestinal tolerance for some patients.

---

## Monitoring Considerations

Patients should maintain appropriate clinical monitoring including:

* Blood glucose assessment as recommended.
* Periodic healthcare reviews.
* Monitoring of treatment effectiveness.

Pharmacists should encourage patients to report:

* Significant changes in tolerance.
* Persistent gastrointestinal concerns.
* Medication-related difficulties.

---

# Lisinopril Patient Management Guidelines

## Therapeutic Purpose

Lisinopril is commonly prescribed for:

* Blood pressure management.
* Cardiovascular risk reduction.
* Certain heart-related conditions.

---

## Administration Principles

Patients should:

* Take medication consistently.
* Avoid irregular dosing patterns.
* Maintain routine monitoring.

---

# Blood Pressure Monitoring Requirements

Patients using antihypertensive therapy should maintain appropriate blood pressure monitoring intervals based on healthcare provider recommendations.

Monitoring considerations include:

* Initial therapy response.
* Dose adjustments.
* Long-term control assessment.

Patients should maintain records where appropriate and communicate significant changes to healthcare professionals.

---

# Chronic Medication Adherence Policy

Pharmacy staff must support adherence through:

* Refill reminders.
* Medication counselling.
* Identification of barriers.
* Referral to healthcare providers when clinical review is required.

Common adherence barriers include:

* Forgetting doses.
* Confusion about instructions.
* Financial limitations.
* Medication side effects.
* Changes in health status.

---

# SECTION 3: REFUND AND INSURANCE POLICIES

# 3.1 Insurance Claim Processing Policy

The pharmacy must process insurance claims according to payer requirements, regulatory obligations, and patient confidentiality standards.

Insurance processing personnel must verify:

* Patient eligibility.
* Insurance information accuracy.
* Prescription claim details.
* Medication coverage status.

---

# 3.2 Insurance Rejection Management Procedure

When an insurance claim is rejected, staff must follow the escalation process below.

---

## Step 1: Identify Rejection Reason

The pharmacy representative must review the rejection message.

Common rejection reasons include:

* Incorrect insurance information.
* Expired coverage.
* Medication not covered.
* Prior authorization requirement.
* Refill too early.
* Quantity limitations.
* Formulary restrictions.

---

## Step 2: Verify Patient Information

Staff must confirm:

* Patient demographic details.
* Insurance identification details.
* Prescription information.
* Prescriber information.

Errors must be corrected before resubmitting claims.

---

## Step 3: Patient Communication

Patients must receive clear information regarding:

* Reason for rejection.
* Available options.
* Expected timelines.
* Required actions.

Staff must avoid providing inaccurate insurance guarantees.

---

## Step 4: Clinical or Administrative Escalation

Cases requiring additional review must be referred to:

* Supervising pharmacist.
* Insurance processing specialist.
* Prescriber when clinical authorization is required.

---

# 3.3 Co-Payment Processing Policy

## Definition

A co-payment represents the patient responsibility amount remaining after insurance contribution according to the applicable coverage plan.

---

## Payment Handling Requirements

Staff must:

* Clearly communicate patient responsibility.
* Provide receipts or transaction records.
* Maintain accurate payment documentation.

---

## Patient Assistance Considerations

Where applicable, staff may provide information regarding:

* Manufacturer assistance programs.
* Alternative covered options.
* Prescriber consultation pathways.

Staff must not alter prescription pricing outside approved procedures.

---

# 3.4 Over-the-Counter Product Return Policy

## Eligible Returns

Unopened over-the-counter products may be accepted for return when:

Requirements:

* Product remains sealed and unused.
* Original packaging is intact.
* Receipt is provided.
* Return occurs within 14 days of purchase.

---

## Return Verification Procedure

Staff must verify:

1. Purchase date.
2. Receipt validity.
3. Product condition.
4. Storage condition.
5. Product identification.

Products showing signs of:

* Opening.
* Damage.
* Tampering.
* Improper storage.

must not be returned to inventory.

---

# 3.5 Prescription Medication Non-Refundable Policy

Prescription medications are generally non-refundable after dispensing due to:

* Patient safety regulations.
* Medication storage integrity concerns.
* Risk of contamination or tampering.
* Regulatory requirements.

---

## Exceptions

Exceptions may only occur when:

* Required by applicable regulation.
* Dispensing error occurred.
* Quality issue is confirmed.
* Manufacturer recall procedure applies.

---

# 3.6 Prescription Return and Disposal Procedures

Returned prescription medications must:

* Not be placed back into pharmacy inventory.
* Be handled according to medication disposal procedures.
* Be documented where required.

Documentation should include:

* Medication name.
* Quantity.
* Reason for disposal.
* Date.
* Responsible staff member.

---

# 3.7 Compliance Documentation Requirements

All refund and insurance activities must maintain accurate records including:

* Patient transaction details.
* Insurance communication history.
* Approval decisions.
* Staff actions.
* Resolution outcomes.

Records must be stored according to organizational retention requirements and privacy regulations.
